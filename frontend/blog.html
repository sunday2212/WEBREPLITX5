NEET PG 2026 Result Declared by NBEMS — General Cut-off at 262 Marks; Scorecards & Answer Keys From October 1

The National Board of Examinations in Medical Sciences (NBEMS) has declared the NEET PG 2026 result following the September 5 re-examination for candidates affected by the Jaipur centre power failure. Qualifying cut-offs have been fixed at 262 marks for General/EWS, 244 for General-PwBD and 226 for reserved categories.

Afrahtafreeh  September 29, 2026  6 min read

The long wait is over. NBEMS has officially declared the NEET PG 2026 result, clearing the way for the post-graduate medical admission season to begin. The declaration came after a re-examination was held on September 5, 2026 for a section of candidates whose exam was disrupted by a power failure at a Jaipur centre — a step NBEMS said was necessary to keep the process fair for every aspirant.

 Key Points

  • General / EWS qualifying cut-off: 262 marks (50th percentile)
  • General-PwBD: 244 marks (45th percentile)
  • SC / ST / OBC (including PwBD): 226 marks (40th percentile)
  • Individual scorecards & official answer keys available on or after October 1, 2026
  • Download portals: nbe.edu.in and natboard.edu.in

The Cut-offs, Explained

NBEMS has set the qualifying percentiles in line with regulation: the 50th percentile for General and EWS candidates translates to 262 marks this year, the 45th percentile for General-PwBD candidates to 244 marks, and the 40th percentile for SC/ST/OBC candidates (including those under PwBD) to 226 marks. Clearing the cut-off makes a candidate eligible for counselling — it does not by itself guarantee a seat, which still depends on rank, category and choices filled during counselling.

Scorecards and Answer Keys From October 1

According to NBEMS, individual scorecards and the official answer keys will be made available for download on or after October 1, 2026 through the candidate portal. The answer key release is expected to include question IDs, response IDs and the evaluation scores, giving candidates a clear view of how their responses were marked.

Clearing the NEET PG cut-off only opens the door to counselling. Your final seat depends on your rank, your category and — crucially — how carefully you fill your choices when MCC opens registration.

— Afrahtafreeh Editorial Desk

What Candidates Should Do Now

  • Download and safely save your scorecard the moment it goes live (October 1 onward)
  • Note your percentile and All-India Rank; compare against last year's closing ranks for your target branch
  • Keep documents ready for counselling — scorecard, admit card, MBBS degree/provisional certificate, internship completion certificate, medical registration and category/PwBD certificates
  • Rely only on official notifications from NBEMS and MCC — avoid unofficial "agents" promising seats

Cut-offs and dates are per the official NBEMS declaration. Always confirm on nbe.edu.in. Sources: NBEMS, Indian Express, The New Indian Express, Careerindia. Last updated: September 29, 2026.

NEET PG 2026 Counselling: MCC Schedule Still Awaited — Full Document Checklist & AIQ Seat Process

With the result out, all eyes are now on the Medical Counselling Committee (MCC), which is yet to release the official NEET PG 2026 counselling schedule. Here's exactly what to prepare so you're not caught off guard when registration opens.

Afrahtafreeh  September 28, 2026  5 min read

The Medical Counselling Committee has not yet announced the dates for NEET PG 2026 counselling. Candidates are advised to monitor the official MCC portal at mcc.nic.in/pg-medical-counselling/ for updates on registration, fee payment, choice filling and the seat matrix. The MCC conducts counselling for 50% of All-India Quota (AIQ) seats, while state authorities handle counselling for their respective state quotas.

 Key Points

  • MCC counselling schedule for NEET PG 2026 is not yet released
  • MCC handles 50% AIQ seats; states run their own quota counselling separately
  • Official portal: mcc.nic.in/pg-medical-counselling/
  • Registration typically involves fee payment, choice filling and seat allotment rounds
  • Keep both AIQ and state-quota options open as a safety strategy

Documents You Must Keep Ready

  • NEET PG 2026 scorecard and admit card
  • MBBS degree certificate / provisional certificate
  • Internship completion certificate
  • Medical registration certificate (permanent/provisional)
  • Category certificate (SC/ST/OBC/EWS), PwBD certificate or NRI documents, where applicable
  • Valid photo ID and recent passport-size photographs

The single biggest avoidable mistake every counselling season is a missing or mismatched certificate. Verify names, dates and spellings across every document now — not on the last day of registration.

— Afrahtafreeh Counselling Desk

How to Approach Choice Filling

Once registration opens, prepare a realistic preference list rather than filling seats emotionally. Study previous years' closing ranks for your target branches and colleges, list a healthy mix of "dream", "realistic" and "safe" options, and never leave the choice list too short. Understand the rules for each round — fresh, upgradation and mop-up — before you lock your choices.

Counselling dates are subject to official MCC notification. Rely only on mcc.nic.in. Sources: MCC, Times of India, Sunday Guardian. Last updated: September 28, 2026.

FMGE October 2026: Medicos Urge NMC & NBE to Grant One-Time Exemption for 2021–22 MBBS-Abroad Batch

Ahead of the October 2026 FMGE, foreign medical graduates from the 2021–22 batch have appealed to the NMC and NBE for a one-time exemption, citing the exceptional disruption caused by the COVID-19 pandemic and the war in Ukraine during their study years.

Afrahtafreeh  September 26, 2026  5 min read

The Foreign Medical Graduate Examination (FMGE) is the mandatory licensing test that every Indian citizen holding a medical degree from a foreign university must clear before being allowed to practise in India. As the October 2026 session approaches, a section of candidates from the 2021–22 MBBS-abroad batch has renewed a long-standing demand: a one-time exemption or relaxation, recognising that their medical education was uniquely disrupted.

 Key Points

  • Candidates from the 2021–22 MBBS-abroad batch are requesting a one-time FMGE exemption
  • They cite COVID-19 online classes and the Ukraine war forcing mid-course transfers
  • The plea is directed at the NMC and NBE ahead of the October 2026 exam
  • As of now, no official policy change has been confirmed
  • FMGE remains twice-yearly; qualifying mark stays 150 out of 300

Why the 2021–22 Batch Is Different

Students who began medicine abroad in 2021–22 faced an unusually turbulent path: prolonged online-only teaching during the pandemic, followed for many by the sudden displacement caused by the war in Ukraine, which forced transfers to universities in other countries mid-degree. Candidates argue that these back-to-back disruptions compromised the clinical exposure that FMGE assumes every graduate has received, and that a one-time relaxation would be a fair acknowledgement of circumstances beyond their control.

We are not asking to skip the standard. We are asking the regulators to recognise that our clinical training was interrupted by a pandemic and a war — events no student could plan around.

— Paraphrased from FMG candidate representations, September 2026

Where the Regulators Stand

So far there has been no confirmed policy change from the NMC or NBE in response to the exemption plea. The regulators have historically maintained that a common licensing standard protects patient safety and keeps the qualification uniform across all foreign graduates. Candidates preparing for October 2026 are therefore advised to continue their preparation on the assumption that the exam will proceed as scheduled, while keeping an eye on official notifications for any relief that may be announced.

This is a developing story; no official exemption has been confirmed. Verify on nmc.org.in and natboard.edu.in. Source: Medical Dialogues. Last updated: September 26, 2026.

NMC Policy September 24, 2026

NMC Releases 2026-27 UG Seat Matrix as Govt Targets 75,000 New Medical Seats — IITs Enter MBBS

The National Medical Commission has published the 2026-27 undergraduate seat matrix and issued directions on mandatory faculty data updates via the AEBAS portal, even as the government pushes an ambitious expansion of medical education capacity.

Afrahtafreeh  September 24, 2026  6 min read

The NMC has released updates on the 2026-27 undergraduate seat matrix and reiterated that colleges must keep faculty attendance and data current on the AEBAS (Aadhaar Enabled Biometric Attendance System) portal — a compliance measure tied directly to how many seats a college is permitted to admit. The moves come against the backdrop of a national push to dramatically expand the number of MBBS and PG seats in the country.

 Key Points

  • NMC has published the 2026-27 UG seat matrix and faculty-data directives
  • Faculty attendance compliance via AEBAS is tied to sanctioned seat capacity
  • The Centre is targeting 75,000 additional medical seats
  • IIT Kanpur, Kharagpur and Madras are building medical schools for "physician-engineers"
  • AIIMS Delhi remains the apex mentor for the country's 23 AIIMS institutions

Why the Seat Matrix Matters to Aspirants

The UG seat matrix is effectively the map of how many seats exist, where, and under which quota — the foundation on which every round of counselling is built. Faculty-data compliance is not a mere formality: colleges that fail to maintain adequate, verifiable faculty on AEBAS risk having their seat intake capped, which can shift the numbers available to students during admissions. For aspirants, a clean, well-populated seat matrix means more predictable counselling.

The 75,000-Seat Ambition and the "Physician-Engineer"

On the policy side, the government has set a target of adding tens of thousands of new medical seats, part of a broader plan to expand the AIIMS network and scale healthcare delivery. In a notable shift, three IITs — Kanpur, Kharagpur and Madras — are actively building medical schools aimed at producing "physician-engineers" who can bridge clinical needs with engineering solutions, from indigenous heart-failure pumps to portable ultrasound devices under development at IIT Kanpur.

India already has the clinical expertise and the technology. What national-scale impact needs now is better alignment between policy on paper and implementation on the ground.

— Theme echoed at the National CVD Summit 2026, Goa

Confirm all seat-matrix details on nmc.org.in. Sources: NMC, India Today, Medical Buyer, Drug Today. Last updated: September 24, 2026.

AIIMS Delhi Transfers India-Trained AI Breast-Cancer Tool to Industry; NIMHANS Launches AI Depression Screening

On its 71st Foundation Day, AIIMS Delhi marked a milestone in indigenous medical AI — transferring an India-trained mammography "second reader" for commercial development — while NIMHANS launched a project to build multilingual AI depression-screening tools.

Afrahtafreeh  September 25, 2026  6 min read

India's medical research landscape in September 2026 is defined by a strong push toward indigenous technology. AIIMS Delhi, which celebrated its 71st Foundation Day on September 25, 2026, has transferred a new AI-based mammography tool to industry for commercial development — a tool trained on Indian data that acts as a "second reader" to assist radiologists in detecting breast cancer.

 Key Points

  • AIIMS Delhi transferred an India-trained AI mammography tool for commercial development
  • It works as a "second reader" assisting radiologists in breast-cancer detection
  • Could reach hospitals within ~6 months, pending regulatory approval
  • NIMHANS + IIT Kharagpur + LGBRIMH launched HEADS for AI depression screening
  • HEADS aims to understand multiple Indian languages

An AI "Second Reader" for Mammograms

Because the mammography model was trained on Indian data, it is tuned to the population it will actually serve — an important factor, since AI tools trained purely on Western datasets can under-perform on Indian patients. Acting as a second reader, it does not replace the radiologist but flags suspicious regions for closer review, helping catch cancers earlier and easing the workload in busy imaging departments. Pending regulatory clearance, the tool could reach hospitals within roughly six months.

HEADS: Mental-Health Screening in Indian Languages

Separately, NIMHANS — in collaboration with IIT Kharagpur and LGBRIMH — launched the HEADS project on September 24, 2026 to develop AI-assisted depression-screening tools capable of understanding multiple Indian languages. Language-aware mental-health screening is a significant step for a country where stigma and a shortage of specialists leave a large mental-healthcare gap, especially outside major cities.

Tools trained on Indian data, for Indian patients, in Indian languages — this is what makes medical AI genuinely useful at national scale, not just in a well-equipped metro hospital.

— Afrahtafreeh Editorial Desk

Regulatory approval timelines are indicative. Sources: Times of India, The Hindu, Drug Today. Last updated: September 25, 2026.

Doctors' Welfare September 22, 2026

After AIIMS Jodhpur Tragedy: 12-Hour Duty Cap & Formal RDA Recognition; AIIMS Nagpur Doctors Protest VIP Culture

The death of a resident doctor at AIIMS Jodhpur has pushed doctor welfare to the top of the agenda, prompting new measures capping continuous duty at 12 hours and formally recognising Resident Doctors' Associations — while doctors at AIIMS Nagpur protest violence and VIP treatment.

Afrahtafreeh  September 22, 2026  5 min read

Administrative and labour challenges are once again in the spotlight across India's premier institutes. Following the death of a resident doctor at AIIMS Jodhpur, the government has implemented new welfare measures aimed at protecting overworked junior doctors — including a limit on continuous duty hours and the formal recognition of Resident Doctors' Associations (RDAs).

 Key Points

  • New welfare measures cap continuous duty at 12 hours
  • Resident Doctors' Associations to be formally recognised
  • Measures follow the death of a resident doctor at AIIMS Jodhpur
  • At AIIMS Nagpur, doctors protested violence against a colleague
  • Nagpur doctors demand a no-special-treatment policy to curb VIP culture

Why Duty-Hour Limits Matter

Punishing shift lengths have long been a defining hardship of residency in India, with junior doctors often working far beyond safe limits. A formal 12-hour cap on continuous duty — alongside recognition of RDAs as legitimate representative bodies — signals a shift toward treating doctor wellbeing as a patient-safety issue, not just an internal HR matter. Fatigue is directly linked to clinical errors, so protecting residents also protects patients.

A doctor who has been awake for 30 hours is not just a tired doctor — they are a safety risk to every patient they treat. Duty-hour reform is patient-safety reform.

— Afrahtafreeh Editorial Desk

Nagpur: Violence and the VIP Problem

Concurrently, doctors at AIIMS Nagpur have protested after an assault on a colleague, demanding stronger protection and a firm "no special treatment" policy to end the VIP culture that pressures staff and distorts care priorities in hospitals. Together, the Jodhpur and Nagpur developments underline a broader theme of September 2026: the people who run India's health system are asking for the same duty of care they extend to patients.

This is a developing story. Sources: Jagran Josh, Swadesi. Last updated: September 22, 2026.
